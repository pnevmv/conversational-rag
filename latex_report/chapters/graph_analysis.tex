\chapter{When the Graph Helps}\label{ch:graphhelp}

On average, adding graph facts to the passages gains about half a point of \fone{}. This
chapter looks at which questions the gain comes from, and whether a cheap router could
choose, per question, between passages, passages with graph facts and graph facts only.
The analysis uses only the cached answers of the full validation split, with the
conversational generator of Chapter~\ref{ch:convgen} (G2 + abstain(G0), \texttt{gpt-5.4-nano});
no new \gls{llm} calls were made and the test split was not used. P denotes passages and E
passages with graph facts. Graph facts only (B) exists only with the current user turn as
input, since it was not run with the dialogue history. For every question
$d = \fone{}(E) - \fone{}(P)$; overall P reaches 27.5\%, E 28.0\% ($d = +0.5$, 95\%
interval $[0.1, 0.9]$) and B 24.1\%.

\section{Gain by Question Type}\label{sec:graphhelp:groups}

Table~\ref{tab:graphhelp:groups} breaks $d$ down by question type; only groups with at
least 100 questions are shown. The clearest split is by retrieval. Where passage retrieval
misses the gold section but at least one fact comes from it (342 questions), E gains 4.6
points, and graph facts alone do as well there (26.2 against 25.2 for E). Where the gold
section is already among the five passages but no fact comes from it, E loses 1.0 point:
the extra facts distract the model from a good context. Over all retrieval hits the gain
is 0.1 points, over all misses 1.3 points.

The other splits show small and spread-out effects. E is ahead on follow-up turns, on
``yes'' replies of the user, when the question names one of the linked entities (a
string match on the entity names) and in the VA domain, and gives nothing on first turns
and in StudentAid. The dialogue act of the reference agent turn, a document switch and the
number of facts make little difference, and every question has ten linked entities, so
their number does not split the questions. Per question, E is better on 48\% and worse on
42\% of the questions, so the average gain is the net of many changes in both directions.

\section{Routing Between Configurations}\label{sec:graphhelp:router}

An oracle router that picks, for each question, the better of P and E with the reference
answer reaches 31.3\% \fone{}, 3.3 points above always using E; adding B as a third
option gives 33.8\%. These upper bounds are mostly noise, however: picking the better of
two runs of the same system E (the cached run and the fresh run of
Section~\ref{sec:convgen:models}) already gains 2.9 points, because two runs of the same
model give different answers to many questions.

A cheap router between P and E was nevertheless tested, with features available before
generation: the cross-encoder scores of the passage ranking (top score, gap between the
first two, mean of the top five), the share of facts whose evidence section is among the
five passages, the mean degree of the linked entities, the number of facts, the position
in the dialogue, the length of the question and whether it names a linked entity. A
logistic regression fitted on dev-500 and evaluated on the other 3{,}927 validation
questions predicts the better configuration in only 52\% of the cases and is 0.5 points
worse than always using E; the best single threshold, on the top reranker score, is 0.2
points worse. The one signal that separates the questions where the facts help, whether a
fact comes from the gold section, depends on the reference answer and is not available at
run time. Always adding the graph facts is therefore the better choice with these signals.
